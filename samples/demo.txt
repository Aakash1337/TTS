# The Nature of Curiosity

Curiosity is often described as the engine of learning. When we encoun-
ter something we don't understand, a small tension forms in the mind, and
that tension seeks resolution.

Researchers have found that curious people retain roughly 30% more of
what they read. See https://example.com/curiosity for the full study.

* It sharpens attention.
* It improves memory.
* It makes the work feel lighter.

In 1984, a psychologist named George Loewenstein framed this as an "inform-
ation gap" — the space between what we already know and what we want to know.
The gap is uncomfortable, and that discomfort is exactly what pulls us forward.
